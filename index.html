About

From logic and language to cells and pipelines.

I studied Computer Science in Ferrara and Artificial Intelligence in Turin. Along the way I worked on Prolog, expert systems, NLP and neural networks, and slowly found myself drawn to biological and biomedical problems. An elective in bioinformatics (sequence alignment, NGS, phylogenetics, gene networks) showed me what computation can do for medicine, and my thesis at the EIDOSLAB research group put it into practice.

Since 2024 I have been a Data Engineer at Multiminds, a Belgian consultancy. I design medallion lakehouses on GCP, batch and near real-time pipelines, and API connectors with secure token-based authentication. I also build AI automation workflows with n8n and LLM agents.

I like international, interdisciplinary teams and hard problems. Long term, I want to work where AI meets bioinformatics.

Research · M.Sc. thesis · 2023

Deep learning on histopathological images for cancer grading.

Pathologists grade colorectal cancer by eye, from how differentiated the tissue looks on a slide. That judgement is subjective. We also know that abnormal microRNA expression drives how this cancer starts and grows.

I trained convolutional networks to compress gigapixel slides into compact descriptions of tumour tissue, then built a second, multimodal model to look for links between those image features and each patient's miRNA profile.

Supervised by Prof. Francesca Cordero, Prof. Marco Grangetto and Dr. Carlo Alberto Barbano, EIDOSLAB, University of Turin.

Read the code on GitHub ↗
  1. Step 1

    Tile the slide

    Whole-slide images cut into patches and filtered with OpenSlide and OpenCV.

  2. Step 2

    CNN grading

    ResNet-18 in PyTorch, used as a classifier and as a feature extractor.

  3. Step 3

    Go multimodal

    Image features paired with each patient's microRNA expression profile.

  4. Step 4

    Select features

    FeatSee, built at the University of Turin, ranks the links between tissue and miRNA.
